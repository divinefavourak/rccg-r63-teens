# 6 — Make It Hooking (the good way)

*(This is a direction from the product owner: the app **should** be hard to put down. Teens today are getting hooked on things that harm them — we want them hooked on something that grows them. This page tells the designer how to do that well.)*

Read this together with `1-look-and-feel.md`. Where a "hook" idea and the calm feeling seem to clash, the trick is almost always to make the hook **warmer and quieter**, not to drop it.

---

## The one big idea: hook the habit, not the session

There are two different things you can pull a teen toward:

- **The session** — "keep scrolling right now, for hours." This is what TikTok is built for.
- **The habit** — "come back every single day, spend a few good minutes, then go and live your life."

**We want the habit.** A daily routine that has a happy ending brings a teen back tomorrow *and* leaves them better. A bottomless feed just wears them out until they delete the app. So we out-hook TikTok on the thing that matters (coming back daily) and we don't try to beat it at the thing that hurts (endless scrolling).

## The daily hook loop

Design the whole app around this simple loop, repeated every day:

1. **A nudge** — a warm notification, or just the habit itself: "today's verse is waiting."
2. **An easy action** — they open the app and the main thing is *right there*, one tap, no digging.
3. **A reward** — something good and a little bit surprising (see below).
4. **They put something in** — a streak grows, a verse is saved, a note is written. The more they invest, the more the app is worth to them, and the more they come back.

Make each step feel effortless and satisfying.

## Steal these from TikTok — use them hard

All of these are good design and fully fit the app. Push them:

- **Instant value on open.** The second the app opens, today's devotional and verse are already there. No loading wall, no menus first.
- **Made for one thumb, zero friction.** Everything important is easy to reach and takes one tap.
- **Short and snackable.** A 4-minute devotional. Short clips. Small wins. Nothing feels like homework.
- **Personal.** Show content that fits this teen — their reading, their next step, their church's events.
- **Buttery motion + instant feedback.** Every tap responds immediately and feels nice. This is a huge part of why TikTok feels good, and it's 100% ours to use.
- **Surprise (variable reward).** They shouldn't know exactly what today holds — maybe a fresh verse, a short story, a challenge, a friend's encouragement, or a milestone. A little "what will today be?" pulls people back.
- **Streaks and progress.** People hate breaking a streak (that's real and powerful) — use it, but always warmly (see the rule below). Show growth: chapters read, days shown up.
- **Smart notifications.** Well-written, value-first nudges that name today's content ("Today: 'When friends change' — 4 min").

## Make "done" feel amazing

This is your secret weapon. Because our experiences have a finish line, you get to give a teen a real hit of "I did it" every single day:

- A satisfying completion moment when the devotional is finished.
- The streak ticks up. A gentle, warm celebration (a leaf unfurling).
- A calm "you're done for today 🌱 — see you tomorrow."

That daily feeling of *finishing something good* is exactly what makes them come back. Chase that, not endless time-in-app.

## The 3 things to avoid (because they backfire)

These aren't banned to be safe — they're banned because they **hurt the numbers and the trust**, especially with teens and their parents:

1. **No bottomless, auto-playing feed.** It hooks the session but burns teens out, and it's TikTok's home turf — we'd lose. Every part of the app gets a **finish line**. End the day's experience on a high, don't trap them in it.
2. **No public popularity contests piled on kids.** Comparison and "who got more likes" is the part of social media that hits teen mental health hardest. *(We are adding likes and reposts to community notes later — that's fine, but keep it moderated and watched, and don't spread popularity numbers onto profiles, friends, or streaks.)*
3. **No guilt or shame to force opens.** "You're falling behind!" gets one open today and a muted app tomorrow. A warm pull ("today's verse is waiting for you") beats a guilt push every time.

## The test for any "hooking" idea

Ask one question:

> **Does this help the teen build a good habit, or does it just trap their attention?**

A streak reminder that says "keep it alive, today's reading is waiting" → builds a habit. Keep it.
An endless feed that auto-plays the next clip → traps attention. Drop it.

Same energy. Opposite result. We want the first one, turned all the way up.
